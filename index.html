Course Overview & Objectives

Welcome to Econ 101: Intermediate Microeconomics! This course builds a rigorous, calculus-based framework to analyze how individual consumers, firms, and markets interact. While introductory economics introduces intuitive concepts, this course focuses on formal economic modeling, optimization techniques, and graphical analyses.

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

  1. Model Consumer Behavior: Derive demand curves using utility maximization subject to budget constraints.
  2. Analyze Producer Decisions: Solve cost-minimization problems and derive supply curves under various production functions.
  3. Evaluate Market Structures: Compare outcomes under perfect competition, monopoly, oligopoly, and game-theoretic models.
  4. Apply Real-World Economics: Interpret current news and policy issues using microeconomic theory.

AI Policy

Problem sets are online, and you may be tempted to lean on AI tools for them. I don’t recommend starting with AI for the entire assignment — it will cost you in the long run for learning, and in the short run for exam grades.

If you need help, please follow this order:

  1. Consult our course tutor.
  2. Come to my office hours.
  3. Then, if needed, use Claude AI — but as a guide, not an answer key. Prompt it to point you toward the right concept rather than to solve the problem for you.

    Example: “Can you tell me which section or concept I should look at to answer this question?”

Problem sets and exams are designed to help you internalize the economic thinking framework that has been developed over the past couple hundred years — that only happens if you do the reasoning yourself.

Disability & Accommodations Policy

Students with a documented disability who require accommodations should register with Accessibility Services (accessibilityservices@cmc.edu, (909) 607-0316) as early as possible. Please note that Accessibility Services’ review process can take up to 15 business days, and accommodations cannot be applied retroactively, so students are strongly encouraged to register well before the semester begins rather than waiting until an exam is near.

Once you receive your Eligibility Letter, please share it with me no later than two weeks before the first exam requiring accommodation, so we have adequate time to arrange logistics together.

For students approved for extended time, exams will be administered through Accessibility Services, since extended time cannot be accommodated within our regular class period. Please contact me at least two weeks in advance of each exam to confirm the specific time and location.
